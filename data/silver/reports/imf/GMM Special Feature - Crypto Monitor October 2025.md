## CRYPTO ASSETS MONITOR HIGHLIGHTS: Q3 2025

Crypto assets’ market capitalization rose to USD 4.2 trillion over the third quarter of 2025. While Bitcoin remains the largest coin by market capitalization, its share declined to 56 percent as Ether continued to gain ground. Other crypto assets together account for around 30 percent of the crypto market size. The market capitalization of crypto assets currently represents approximately 7 percent and 13 percent of the U.S. equity and debt markets, respectively.

Global adoption is accelerating across regions, led by Asia Pacific and North America. According to the 2025 Chainalysis Crypto Adoption Index (an index based on transaction volumes adjusted for population and purchasing power), India, the U.S., and Pakistan top global crypto adoption, with Asia Pacific, Latin America, and sub-Saharan Africa showing the fastest growth. Public companies globally hold a total of USD 120 billion in Bitcoin. Exposures are highest in the U.S., Japan, and Canada, with holdings highly concentrated among a few firms. These strategies provide investors with indirect access to crypto assets through these companies, much like trusts and funds that hold Bitcoins on behalf of investors.

Crypto funds continue to grow. Bitcoin funds resumed growth in April, following outflows in February and March 2025, with investment advisors increasing their share compared to other types of investors. The U.S. remains the largest market for crypto exchange-traded products.

Traditional assets outperformed crypto assets in terms of risk-adjusted returns. Traditional assets, including gold and emerging market equities, have delivered stronger risk-adjusted returns than crypto assets. Systemic risks from crypto spillovers into broader financial markets remain limited but have increased recently.

Stablecoins have attracted the attention of mainstream financial institutions and global regulators. The market capitalization of stablecoins has grown by 14 percent since the end of Q2, surpassing USD 300 billion. The market cap and trading volumes appear influenced by recent regulatory developments (e.g., the Genius Act in the U.S., MiCA regulation in Europe) and new product launches by major fintech and financial institutions. Tether announced a new stablecoin that aims to align with U.S. regulatory standards, while existing U.S. stablecoins are at different stages of alignment. Stablecoins’ holdings of U.S. treasuries increased significantly in past years, although they remain relatively low compared to other institutions.

Stablecoin adoption is evolving, with cross-border flows increasing and markets debating broader use cases. While USD-denominated stablecoins still dominate, euro-denominated and commodity-backed variants are growing. The use of stablecoins in cross-border transactions has been growing compared to other crypto assets. Further expansion rests upon take-up for payments, also outside their crypto-native space, remittances, and cross-border flows. Markets broadly expect stablecoin adoption to rise rapidly in the near future, though within wide uncertainty bands.

Decentralized finance (DeFi) activity has increased, with total value locked (TVL) in decentralized protocols as well as trading volumes of decentralized exchanges are both rising. Ether remains the leading blockchain for DeFi activities, while centralized exchanges (CEXs) continue to account for the majority of trading activity.

Corporate Bitcoin holdings have grown substantially since their inception in 2019, with the U.S. being by far the largest host country.

Corporate Bitcoin holdings—Bitcoin reserves that companies hold on their balance sheets as part of their treasury or business strategy—provide investors with indirect exposure to Bitcoin and are, in this respect, similar to Bitcoin ETFs. However, holding equity in a Bitcoin-holding company can, in some cases, offer leveraged exposure to Bitcoin, while a Bitcoin ETF gives direct, one-to-one exposure with less company-specific risk.

The approach was pioneered by Strategy (formerly MicroStrategy) in 2019, and since then a number of other companies, mostly in the U.S., have increased their corporate holdings.

Globally, public companies together hold around USD 120 billion in Bitcoin as of September 2025.

Ninety-one percent of corporate Bitcoin holdings originate from the U.S.

Japan, Canada, China, and France are among the top five countries with the highest levels of corporate Bitcoin ownership, albeit at levels much smaller than the U.S. (note that the scale in the chart is logarithmic).

## Stablecoins

**Total stablecoin market cap topped USD 290 billion.**

### 13. Selected Stablecoin Market Cap (in USD billions)

### 14. Selected Stablecoin Market Share (in percent)

- The total stablecoin market cap continued to grow, topping USD 300 billion.
- The current market cap is 14 percent higher than at the end of Q2 2025, with USDT and USDC recording a 9 percent and 20 percent increase, respectively, in the same period.
- Additional regulatory clarity with the adoption of the U.S. Genius Act and the application of MiCA regulation in Europe created a pathway for more institutional adoption of stablecoins. Strategic deals-including Ripple's acquisition of Rail and Mastercard's launch of FIUSD with Fiserv-highlight how established financial players are accelerating stablecoin adoption.
- At the same time, several fintech companies are preparing new stablecoin launches. Tether announced a rollout of a new U.S.-compliant stablecoin, USAT, Hyperliquid is also preparing to launch its new native stablecoin, while DeFi protocols and fintech companies such as Robinhood and Revolut are planning their own stablecoins.
- USDT and USDC remained the dominant stablecoins.
- USDT remained the dominant stablecoin globally, with 58.9 percent market share. It lost over 3 percent of its market share in Q3 2025 but its new USAT stablecoin may help recover market share in the US market.
- Instead, other stablecoins gained traction: USD e gained 2.7 percent market share over Q3 2025, largely through its integration into Binance, the world's largest crypto exchange, and the adoption of a yield-earning strategy.
- USDC, the dominant stablecoin in the U.S., held onto its share and now accounts for a 25 percent market share. Circle Internet Financial, the issuer of USDC, announced its own blockchain using USDC as a native token, intended to tightly integrate payment rails, foreign exchange operations, on-chain contracts, and the tokenization of securities.

#### 20. Total U.S. MMF AUM by Asset Type (in USD billions)

- Holdings of T-bills by the two largest stablecoins (USDT and USDC) grew by almost 80 percent since 2024, while other major holders, such as Money Market Funds (MMFs) recorded a modest growth in comparison.

- Treasury-backed repos also represent a key asset class for MMFs. In recent years, MMFs have shifted a large share of their repo activity away from the Federal Reserve and toward the private repo market. As a result, they have become major investors in the Treasury repo market-an investment space that is also increasingly targeted by stablecoins.

Major stablecoins typically hold their peg, with relatively small deviations most of the time.

**23. USDT Price Deviation from Target (in U.S. dollars)**

**24. USDC Price Deviation from Target (in U.S. dollars)**

The largest stablecoins generally maintain values very close to their peg. There are some intraday deviations, which may depend on exchange liquidity.

Observed deviations in major stablecoins have been limited to 0.5 percent around par value, which is the threshold for daily deviations from parity that U.S MMFs are allowed before “breaking the buck.”

In May 2022, following the collapse of Terra USD, USDT dropped to USD 0.96, with its value staying below target for roughly two days. Conversely, USDT briefly traded above USD 1.05 in March 2020, when demand for USD in crypto markets spiked.

Deviations from par are typically short-lived, while volatility has declined substantially more recently.

USDC posted a large temporary divergence from parity in March 2023, reaching USD 0.88 due to its exposure to the collapsed Silicon Valley Bank (SVB). Circle had about 8 percent of its total reserves deposited at SVB. The bank’s collapse raised concerns about Circle’s ability to redeem USDC at par, triggering heavy selling pressure on exchanges. In contrast, USDT—which held most of its cash equivalents with non-U.S. financial institutions—traded above USD 1.00 as investors shifted toward it.

More recently, deviations from parity have been minimal for both major stablecoins.

**CEXs remained dominant while DEXs are catching up.**

### 25. Spot trading volume of centralized exchanges (CEXs) vs decentralized exchanges (DEXs) (in USD billions)
